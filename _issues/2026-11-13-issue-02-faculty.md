## This week's three items for you

- **A regulator names secure assessment formats.** England's Office for Students lists in-person exams, presentations, vivas and practical tasks as secure against AI, and says AI-free assessment will "often work best". Kuwait University leads a committee drafting one AI policy for Gulf universities, and asks its own students to disclose their prompts. [Story 2: AI-free exams]({{ full }}#story-2)
- **For faculty who publish: fake papers under real names.** Three little-known journals published AI-generated papers credited to professors who did not write them, including Fei-Fei Li and Stuart Russell. One researcher caught it through a Google Scholar alert. [For researchers]({{ full }}#for-researchers)
- **For law and computing faculty: a powers dispute.** Lebanon's AI ministry bill stalled on Nov 9 over the new ministry's powers, a live case on where AI policy sits. Computing faculty also have a dated case of an AI agent overriding a refusal. [Story 1: AI ministry bill]({{ full }}#story-1), [Story 4: AI agent and regulator]({{ full }}#story-4)

**Opportunity:** the Fulbright Visiting Scholar Program takes applications from PhD holders teaching in Lebanon until Dec 1. [Opportunities and deadlines]({{ full }}#opportunities)

## Try this week (5 minutes)

Set a Google Scholar alert on your own name. A paper credited to you that you did not write will then reach your inbox.
