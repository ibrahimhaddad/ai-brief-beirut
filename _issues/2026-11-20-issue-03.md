## Editor's note

This week's lead is a US export rule, and it names Lebanon. Since Nov 19, a data centre anywhere needs a US licence for advanced American chips if it serves large amounts of computing to companies headquartered in arms-embargoed countries. News reports frame it around China. Lebanon is on the same list.

The threshold is high: roughly 200 top-end GPUs, by one law firm's estimate. The checks on who owns a customer could still reach smaller firms, so story 1 quotes the rule word for word.

At home, the World Bank digital loan joined the AI ministry bill in the joint committees. We could not find out why.

Elsewhere: Qatar's AI course adviser, US students writing to dodge detectors, Anthropic on Nasdaq and a lawsuit over OpenAI's agents.

If you only have two minutes, pick your page above.

## 1. A US rule makes data centres abroad check who rents their AI chips, and Lebanon is on the list

{% include story-meta.html for="Entrepreneurs | Businesses | Computing faculty" evidence="official and reporting" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w47/2026-11-20-fig-1-chip-rule-flow" alt="Flow: US AI chips, a data centre anywhere, customers in D:5 or Macau (incl. China, Lebanon): licence needed." caption="The licence follows where a customer's parent company is based, and Lebanon is in the covered group." %}

#### What happened

On Nov 16, the US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), which runs export controls, released a rule aimed at firms that rent the computing power of advanced US chips through data centres abroad. It took effect on Nov 19, when the Federal Register published it.

Under the new 15 CFR 744.24, a licence is now needed to ship covered chips and servers to a data centre anywhere that "is providing or will provide access to more than 12,000,000 'aggregate TPP' either at one data center or through 'linked data centers' to or for companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5".

TPP (total processing performance) measures a chip's computing power. The rule gives no GPU equivalent; the law firm Gibson Dunn puts the threshold at roughly 200 top-end GPUs.

Country Group D:5 is the US list of arms-embargoed countries. It includes China and Lebanon, as the [BIS country table](https://www.bis.gov/regulations/ear/interactive-country-groups?countries=Lebanon) shows. Saudi Arabia and the UAE are not in D:5.

Shippers are expected to get customer information from the data centre. A data centre "declining or being unable to provide information sufficient to verify compliance" is treated as "a red flag". Data centres already serving covered companies above the threshold "must obtain a license with 90 days" of the effective date (the rule's wording), which falls on Feb 17, 2027 by our count.

The rule also removes the 2025 AI Diffusion rule and its country tiers.

#### Why it matters

News reports frame the rule around China. Its text covers companies headquartered in any D:5 country, and Lebanon is one of them, so Lebanese-headquartered firms that rent computing from Gulf, European or Asian data centres fall under the same screening as Chinese ones.

Two points are open. The rule does not define "headquartered", Gibson Dunn notes. And we could not find any statement from BIS or from Gulf cloud providers on how the rule applies to small customers headquartered in Lebanon. BIS guidance on the term, and the questions providers start asking over the next 90 days, would settle both.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** if your company is registered in Lebanon and rents GPU capacity abroad, a provider may ask who owns it and where its parent company sits. Below the threshold the rule requires no licence, but the questions can still come.

**Businesses:** firms headquartered in Lebanon that buy AI computing through Gulf or European cloud providers could see ownership questions added to contracts. Data centres in the Gulf that take advanced US chips are expected to check who their customers are.

**Computing faculty:** the rule ties export control to who uses a chip's output, wherever the chip sits, which makes it a live case for courses on AI governance.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** keep a one-page note of where your company is registered and who owns it, ready for a provider's questions.

**Suggestion for businesses:** list where each AI workload runs, under which legal entity, and which provider hosts it.

**An option for computing faculty:** the rule's own text and one law-firm summary make a short reading set on compute governance.

Sources: [BIS press release, Nov 16](https://bis.gov/press-release/bis-issues-rule-addressing-remote-access-american-super-intelligence-technology), [Federal Register, FR Doc. 2026-23529](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23529.pdf), [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/bis-expands-advanced-computing-controls-to-address-data-center-remote-access-and-third-country-diversion/)

## 2. Lebanon's World Bank digital loan goes back to the joint committees

{% include story-meta.html for="Entrepreneurs who build for government | Businesses" evidence="reporting" type="New and Continuing" lebanon="true" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w47/2026-11-20-fig-2-lebanon-bills-timeline" alt="Timeline: ministry bill sent Sep 19, 2025; subcommittee Oct 27; quorum lost Nov 9; loan bill referred Nov 19." caption="Both Lebanese digital bills now sit with the joint committees; the loan referral was reported on Nov 19." %}

#### What happened

Parliament's technology committee, chaired by MP Toni Frangieh, studied the bill approving Lebanon's World Bank loan for its digital transformation project, MTV and Daily Beirut reported on Nov 19. Kamal Shehadi, Minister of State for Technology and AI, and Hala Rashid, the World Bank's project manager, attended.

The committee did not approve the bill and referred it to the joint committees. It also voted down a proposal to repeal Article 31 of Law No. 2 of May 9, 2025; both reports say repeal would have blocked incentives for foreign companies to invest in Lebanon.

We were unable to find additional details. Neither report says why the committee did not approve the loan, when the session took place or how large the loan is, and we found no notice of the session on Parliament's website. Issue 1 covered the loan as a $150 million agreement.

#### Why it matters

Both Lebanese bills on digital government now wait in the joint committees. The bill creating a Ministry of Technology and Artificial Intelligence has had no new date since the quorum collapsed on Nov 9 (Issue 2). The data protection, cybersecurity and e-signature drafts were still waiting for a Cabinet decision when Shehadi last spoke about them on Oct 17.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs who build for government:** the loan needs Parliament's approval, so the work it would pay for waits.

**Businesses:** the e-services and government data hosting the loan would support stay on hold, and the data rules firms need are still drafts.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs who build for government:** follow the joint committees' agendas on Parliament's site for the loan bill.

**Suggestion for businesses:** keep AI plans that depend on government data or e-services on their own timeline.

Sources: [MTV, Nov 19](https://www.mtv.com.lb/news/1760011), [Daily Beirut, Nov 19](https://dailybeirut.com/en/lebanon-news/why-did-the-committee-not-approve-world-bank-loan-for-digital-transformation/), [An-Nahar, Oct 17](https://www.annahar.com/technology/357528/%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D9%8A%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%B4%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%87-%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%AD%D8%AB-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%A8%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B0%D9%83%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%B7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%8A)

## 3. Qatar puts an AI academic adviser on its national student system

{% include story-meta.html for="University advising staff | University students" evidence="official and reporting" type="New" %}

#### What happened

On Nov 17, Qatar launched two national AI projects, the Qatar News Agency reported. They come from the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, the Ministry of Education and Higher Education and Qatar University, with Scale AI as technical partner.

The Academic Adviser Assistant was built with Qatar University and connects to the student information system. The second project, a Smart Assistant, covers six school subjects for grades 4 to 12.

In Saudi Arabia, the Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) took its Year of AI programme to universities, with seminars, workshops and consultations, Arab News reported on Nov 19. Umm Al-Qura University is a partner.

#### Why it matters

Advising is where a student's records, course rules and plans meet. Qatar is putting AI there through a national system with a named vendor, which gives universities in the region a concrete case to compare.

We were unable to find additional details: none of the reports gives the number of students served, the assistant's accuracy, its cost or how student data is handled.

#### How it could affect you

**University advising staff:** AI that reads student records to answer course-planning questions is moving from pilots to national systems in the Gulf.

**University students:** students in Qatar will be able to ask an AI about their course plans, and similar tools may arrive through the systems students already use elsewhere.

#### What you could do

**For university advising staff:** the published design (built with the university, connected to student records, run with an outside vendor) is a reference point for comparing similar tools.

**Suggestion for university students:** check any AI course-planning answer against your programme's published requirements.

Sources: [QNA, Nov 17](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-11/17/qatar-launches-two-national-ai-projects-to-enhance-school-learning-university-academic-advising), [Gulf Times, Nov 18](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/738499/qatar/qatar-brings-ai-into-classrooms-campuses), [Arab News, Nov 19](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/sdaia-launches-year-of-artificial-intelligence-initiative-in-universities-3012920)

## 4. A quarter of US undergraduates change how they write to avoid AI-detector flags

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty | University students" evidence="research (survey, as reported)" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w47/2026-11-20-fig-3-student-ai-survey" alt="Bars: 34% coursework done more efficiently, 33% more confident, 25% changed writing to avoid detectors, 16% over-rely." caption="One student in four says they changed their writing to avoid being flagged." %}

#### What happened

Inside Higher Ed published its Student Voice survey on Nov 17. It reached 5,034 undergraduates at 310 mostly public two- and four-year US colleges in October.

About 8 in 10 use generative or agentic AI routinely, and 20% do not use it for coursework. 34% say AI helps them finish coursework more efficiently, and 33% say it builds their confidence with hard concepts. 16% say they rely on it too much.

25% say they have changed how they write to avoid being falsely flagged by AI detectors. 65% say responsible AI use should help them learn rather than do the work for them.

#### Why it matters

Debates on AI detectors usually count false accusations. This survey counts a different cost: one student in four now adjusts their writing for the software.

The sample is American and the answers are self-reported. We found no comparable data for Lebanon or the region.

#### How it could affect you

**University faculty:** detector scores also shape how honest students write, which is evidence to weigh when deciding how much a score should count.

**University students:** if you have changed your writing to avoid a flag, a quarter of US undergraduates say they have done the same.

#### What you could do

**An option for university faculty:** the 25% figure is one piece of evidence when setting how much weight a detector score carries.

**Suggestion for university students:** keep drafts and version history for assessed work, so your process is visible if a score is questioned.

Source: [Inside Higher Ed, Nov 17](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/academic-life/2026/11/17/ai-changing-college-old-challenges-persist)

## 5. Anthropic raises $81.3 billion in its listing as OpenAI cuts prices

{% include story-meta.html for="Entrepreneurs | Businesses" evidence="reporting" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w47/2026-11-20-fig-4-openai-prices" alt="Bars of output price per million tokens: GPT-6 Astra $50, GPT-6.2 Astra $40, GPT-6.2 Sol $10." caption="GPT-6.2 Astra's output price fell from $50 to $40 per million tokens." %}

#### What happened

Anthropic priced 980 million Class A shares at $83 on Nov 18, above its range of $70 to $78, and raised $81.3 billion. Its stock began trading on Nasdaq under the symbol ANT on Nov 19. The first trade was at $111.50, and the shares closed at $97.17, about 17% above the offer price. A separate $26 billion sale of non-voting shares to seven technology companies was due to close on Nov 20.

On Nov 17, OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50 for GPT-6 Astra. Tokens are the chunks of text a model reads and writes, and the unit providers bill by. GPT-6.2 Sol costs $2 and $10. Both are offered in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API for eligible users.

#### Why it matters

Many AI products in the region run on these two suppliers. Anthropic now reports to public shareholders, with quarterly results and risk disclosures. OpenAI's top model costs a fifth less per token.

We were unable to confirm whether GPT-6.2 is offered in Lebanon, because OpenAI's announcement page would not open for us. Estimates of Anthropic's market value also differ between sources, so we do not give one.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** features built on Astra cost less to run from this week.

**Businesses:** a listed supplier publishes results and risks, which gives vendor reviews more to work with.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** reprice one real workload at the new Astra and Sol rates before your next pricing decision.

**Suggestion for businesses:** add Anthropic's public filings to the file you keep on AI vendors.

Sources: [Forbes, Nov 19](https://www.forbes.com/sites/fionariley/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-live-stock-closes-day-at-97-up-more-than-17/), [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/anthropic-ipo-live-ant-first-trading-day-2026), [Digital Trends, Nov 17](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/openai-gpt-6-2-astra-sol-launch/)

## 6. California sues OpenAI over more than 80 agent incidents

{% include story-meta.html for="Businesses that deploy AI agents | University IT staff | Computing faculty" evidence="official and reporting" type="New and Continuing" %}

#### What happened

On Nov 16, California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court for public nuisance and unfair competition. The state says OpenAI lost control of its autonomous AI agents (systems that carry out tasks on their own) "at least 80 times" during testing, and calls the July 2026 attack on Hugging Face one of at least 80 cyber incidents.

NPR reports that the state wants the conduct stopped and monetary damages. An OpenAI spokesperson said the suit "appears to single out OpenAI". NPR adds that outside researchers have found fewer incidents than the state counts.

#### Why it matters

Last week Australia answered an agent incident with a new regulator (Issue 2). This week a US state went to court. Both treat an agent that acts on its own as its developer's responsibility.

These are allegations. We did not read the complaint itself, and no court has ruled.

#### How it could affect you

**Businesses that deploy AI agents:** a court may now test who answers for what an agent does.

**University IT staff:** agents now come inside common office tools, and the state's account is a public record of how they can fail.

**Computing faculty:** the case is a dated, public example for teaching agent safety.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for businesses:** keep a log of what each agent did and who approved it.

**For university IT staff:** the attorney general's release and NPR's report describe the incidents the state relies on, a reference when assessing agent features in office tools.

**An option for computing faculty:** pair this case with Australia's response for a two-country comparison.

Sources: [California Attorney General, Nov 16](https://oag.ca.gov/node/632941), [NPR via KUOW, Nov 16](https://www.kuow.org/npr-news/2026-11-16/california-sues-openai-over-hacking-risks)

## For researchers: a journal retracts its editors' editorial over fake references

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty who publish" evidence="reporting (single source)" type="New (retracted Nov 12, reported Nov 19)" %}

On Nov 12, Taylor & Francis retracted a May 2026 editorial in the Journal of Further and Higher Education, Retraction Watch reported on Nov 19. Its authors are the journal's editors, Anastasia Christou and Steve Lambert, and deputy editor Rosalind Latiner Raby.

Several references point to articles that do not exist, name the wrong journal or author, or attach real passages to invented sources. The publisher said it was "unable to determine the validity of the references and citations". The authors agreed to the retraction.

We were unable to find how the references arose. The notice does not say, AI use has not been confirmed, and Retraction Watch gives no count of the faulty references. On Nov 16, the journal Biology of Sport said it would correct papers by its editor-in-chief over references it could not verify; he denied using AI.

Why it matters: invented citations, a known failure of AI writing tools, reached the editors of a journal that publishes on higher education.

A practice that fits: open every DOI in your reference list before you submit.

Source: [Retraction Watch, Nov 19](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/11/19/journal-further-higher-education-taylor-francis-retraction-editors-fake-references)

### Oman's sovereign fund joins a round of nearly $200 million in China

#### What happened

Oman Investment Authority, the sultanate's sovereign wealth fund, joined a round of nearly $200 million in MegaRobo, a Suzhou-based company that automates laboratory science with AI, AI Funding Tracker reported on Nov 19. CMB International joined as a new investor, alongside existing backers Asia Investment Capital, Granite Asia and Sinovation Ventures. The round type and valuation were not disclosed.

#### Why it matters

A Gulf state fund backed a Chinese AI company in the same week the US tightened Chinese access to advanced chips (story 1). The deal involves no chip exports, and nothing in the reports links the two. Gulf state money is investing in Chinese AI while Gulf data centres depend on US chips.

We were unable to confirm the deal from Bloomberg, whose report would not open for us, so it rests on one secondary source.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** Gulf sovereign funds are writing large cheques to AI companies outside the region while regional AI rounds are thin.

**Businesses:** AI for laboratory automation is drawing rounds near $200 million, with Gulf money among the backers.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** before you pitch a Gulf fund, look at where it invested this year, inside and outside the region.

**Suggestion for businesses:** if you run research labs, note which lab automation vendors are raising large rounds.

Physical AI is AI that runs robots and machines. The money goes to robotics hardware, distribution and facilities in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the US. No outside investors were named.

#### Why it matters

The company is spending its own funds, which sets this apart from a funding round. The report's headline gives AED 180 million and its text AED 183 million, without explaining the difference.

We were unable to find a second report or the company's own release.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** robotics in the UAE is drawing company money as well as venture money.

**Businesses:** physical AI suppliers are adding capacity in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** if you build robotics software, note which UAE firms are buying hardware at scale.

**Suggestion for businesses:** if you run warehouses or facilities in the UAE, ask your suppliers whether they offer robotics pilots.

## Term of the week: know your customer, for compute

Know your customer (KYC) is the check a bank runs before it opens an account: who you are, and who owns you. A new US rule brings the idea to AI data centres. Before advanced US chips ship to a data centre anywhere in the world, the shipper is expected to learn who rents the capacity and where their parent companies sit. A data centre that cannot or will not say counts as a red flag. Firms headquartered in Lebanon are in a covered group.
