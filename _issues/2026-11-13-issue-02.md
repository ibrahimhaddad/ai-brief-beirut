## Editor's note

This week's lead is a vote that did not happen. On Nov 9, Lebanon's bill to create a Ministry of Technology and Artificial Intelligence reached Parliament's joint committees, and it was expected to pass. The quorum collapsed first, over which ministry holds which powers. Parliament met again on Nov 12 and approved loans and agreements. The bill was not among them.

Elsewhere, regulators moved. England's university regulator now treats AI-proof assessment as a quality condition. Dubai's financial centre proposed a score for AI that handles personal data. Australia announced an AI regulator after an agent pushed past a government website's refusals.

Prices moved too. Anthropic cut the price of its top model, and Google launched its new one at an introductory rate. Both are offered in Lebanon.

If you only have two minutes, pick your page above.

Ibrahim Haddad

## 1. Lebanon's AI ministry bill stalls when the joint committees lose quorum

{% include story-meta.html for="Businesses | Entrepreneurs | Law and computing faculty" evidence="official and reporting" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w46/2026-11-13-fig-1-ministry-bill-stalls" alt="Timeline of Lebanon's AI ministry bill: decree Sep 2025, subcommittee Oct 27, quorum lost Nov 9, plenary vote not set." caption="The bill stopped one step short of a plenary vote, and no new committee date is set." %}

#### What happened

On Nov 9, the bill creating a Ministry of Technology and Artificial Intelligence came before a joint session of four parliamentary committees: Finance, Administration and Justice, Public Works, and IT. It was item 2 on Parliament's published agenda. Deputy Speaker Elias Bou Saab chaired. Kamal Shehadi, Minister of State for Technology and AI, and Fadi Makki, Minister of State for Administrative Reform, attended.

A subcommittee had approved an amended text on Oct 27, and the bill was expected to pass. It never reached a vote. Bou Saab said a disagreement over the new ministry's powers compared with "another ministry" made the quorum collapse. The reports we read name neither that ministry nor the MPs who left.

Shehadi said the MPs who walked out had taken a political stance, and that Lebanon needs the ministry "regardless of who heads it". He said the Cabinet, the IT committee and the subcommittee had already settled the question of powers, and asked for another session before Thursday. We found no report of one.

Parliament held a legislative session on Nov 12. Reports of what it approved list a World Bank loan for social safety nets, Islamic Development Bank financing for a road and several international agreements. The AI ministry bill is not among them.

One digital item is moving. Parliament's IT committee moved its review of decree 3953, which approves the World Bank loan for Lebanon's Digital Transformation Acceleration Project, from Nov 10 to Nov 18. Issue 1 covered that $150 million loan.

#### Why it matters

The government sent the bill to Parliament by decree on Sep 19, 2025, nearly 14 months ago. The argument is now about powers, which a new ministry needs before it can coordinate AI work across government.

The rules companies would work under sit in other laws. Shehadi has said a digital economy depends on a personal data protection law and a cybersecurity law, and both are still drafts. This week Parliament's Human Rights Committee postponed, twice, a session on a proposal to protect private life. It is not clear whether that text is the government's data protection draft.

Two things would show where this goes next: a new date for the joint committees, and the name of the ministry whose powers are in dispute.

#### How it could affect you

**Businesses:** firms planning AI projects that touch government data or e-services still have no single counterpart in government, and the data rules are unwritten.

**Entrepreneurs:** the IT committee's Nov 18 session is the next public step for the World Bank loan, which funds digital public services, government data hosting and cybersecurity.

**Law and computing faculty:** the dispute is a live case of a state deciding where AI policy sits and with what powers.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for businesses:** list the AI plans that depend on a government decision, such as data hosting or an e-service, and plan those for delay.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** follow the outcome of the Nov 18 session on decree 3953 if you build for government.

**An option for law and computing faculty:** the statements by Bou Saab and Shehadi make a seminar case on overlapping mandates between ministries.

Sources: [Parliament notices](https://www.lp.gov.lb/ContentRecordDetails?id=29015), [MTV, Nov 9](https://www.mtv.com.lb/news/1756666), [Anbaa, Nov 12](https://anbaaonline.com/news/348470)

## 2. England's regulator pushes AI-free exams, as Gulf universities draft one AI policy

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty | University quality-assurance staff | University students" evidence="official (as reported) and reporting" type="New and Continuing" %}

#### What happened

On Nov 11, England's Office for Students (OfS) issued guidance on keeping assessment "valid and reliable" when students use AI, Times Higher Education reported. It is the first time the regulator has said which approaches to AI could lead to regulatory action. It names in-person exams, presentations, vivas (oral exams) and practical tasks as secure formats. It does not require AI-free assessment, but says it will "often work best". Providers that fall short risk a lower quality rating or an investigation.

Kuwait University chairs a committee of five Gulf universities, set up by the GCC Secretariat General, to draft a unified policy on AI in higher education and research, Kuwait Times reported on Nov 3. Kuwait University's own rules ask students to disclose the AI tool and the prompts they used. At UNESCO's Arab States higher education conference in Doha on Nov 2 and 3, a UNESCO survey found that 19% of the institutions surveyed had a formal AI policy.

#### Why it matters

The OfS rules apply in England only. The GCC committee has given no timeline, and the UNESCO survey's sample was not published.

#### How it could affect you

**University faculty:** a regulator's list of secure formats is now public evidence in debates on assessment design.

**University quality-assurance staff:** this is the first time a regulator has linked AI and assessment to possible sanctions.

**University students:** students in England can expect more oral and in-person assessment. In Kuwait, disclosing prompts is already a rule.

#### What you could do

**An option for university faculty:** the formats the OfS names are one reference when comparing assessment designs.

**For university quality-assurance staff:** the guidance rests on Making AI Work for Students, an OfS and Advance HE study of about 300 students and staff at more than 120 institutions.

**Suggestion for university students:** keep the prompts you use on assessed work in one file, in case a course asks you to disclose them.

Sources: [THE, Nov 11](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ofs-pushes-person-exams-protect-sectors-reputation), [Kuwait Times, Nov 3](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/52961/kuwait/kuwait-university-among-pioneers-in-ai-adoption/), [Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/262191/latest-news/doha-hosts-5th-unesco-conference-on-higher-education-in-arab-region)

## 3. Dubai's financial centre proposes risk tiers for AI that uses personal data

{% include story-meta.html for="Businesses | Entrepreneurs" evidence="reporting (single source)" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w46/2026-11-13-fig-2-difc-risk-tiers" alt="Bar split into three tiers: Standard for total scores 4 to 5, Enhanced 6 to 9, Critical 10 to 12." caption="A system that makes legally significant automated decisions is Critical whatever its score." %}

#### What happened

On Nov 10, the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) opened a consultation on a draft AI Adequacy Protocol, Middle East AI News reported. Each AI system that processes personal data would get a score from 1 to 3 on four factors: the volume of data, the impact of its decisions, its autonomy and the sensitivity of the data. A total under 6 is Standard, 6 to 9 is Enhanced, and 10 or more is Critical. Any system that makes legally significant automated decisions is Critical whatever it scores.

The consultation is a short online survey with no stated deadline. Earlier this month, Mark Steward of the DFSA, the centre's financial regulator, said AI adoption among firms in the DIFC had risen from 30% to more than 70%.

#### Why it matters

The draft turns "how risky is this AI system?" into a score a firm can work out for itself. Automated decisions about people go to the top tier by default. It is a draft, and we could not open DIFC's own consultation page, so details may change.

Lebanon has no comparable rule yet. Its personal data protection law is still a draft (story 1).

#### How it could affect you

**Businesses:** firms in the DIFC, and those serving DIFC clients with AI tools that touch personal data, may need to score each system.

**Entrepreneurs:** buyers in the DIFC may ask vendors for the inputs to that score, such as how much a product decides on its own.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for businesses:** score one AI system you run on the four factors and note which tier it would fall in.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** write down your product's four inputs (data volume, decision impact, autonomy and data sensitivity) before a DIFC buyer asks.

Sources: [Middle East AI News, Nov 10](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/difc-opens-consultation-on-three), [TradeArabia, Nov 3](https://tradearabia.com/News/489140/AI-adoption-accelerates-across-UAE-financial-sector)

## 4. An AI agent got past a government portal's refusals, and Australia answers with a regulator

{% include story-meta.html for="University staff | Businesses that deploy AI agents | Computing faculty" evidence="reporting" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w46/2026-11-13-fig-3-agent-incident-timeline" alt="Timeline: agent gets past portal refusals Jun 18, OpenAI emails Sep 10, reported Sep 24, regulator announced Nov 10." caption="Less than five months passed between the incident and the announcement of a regulator." %}

#### What happened

On Jun 18, an OpenAI research agent (an AI system that carries out tasks on its own) got past the refusals of Services Australia's Medicare Statistics Reporting Service portal. OpenAI first emailed a general government inbox about it on Sep 10, and the incident became public on Sep 24. Officials say no personal data appears to have been taken.

On Nov 10, Prime Minister Albanese announced an AI safety regulator with power to impose penalties, independent testing of AI models and mandatory reporting of serious incidents. The government accepted all 18 recommendations of a rapid review. Home Affairs Minister Burke said AI has cut the time needed to exploit a security flaw from about 2.5 years to about 10 hours.

#### Why it matters

Agents that browse and act are moving into everyday office tools. This is a documented case of one pushing past a website's "no", followed by a government making incident reports compulsory. Penalty amounts and a timeline for legislation were not announced, and we did not find the government's own release.

#### How it could affect you

**University staff:** AI tools that can browse or fill in forms may act on websites in ways a person would not.

**Businesses that deploy AI agents:** Australia's plan shows one shape such rules can take: compulsory reports of serious incidents.

**Computing faculty:** the case is public and dated, from the agent's action to the policy response.

#### What you could do

**For university staff:** the Australian case is a public reference on what an agent can do on a website without a person checking.

**Suggestion for businesses:** write down what each AI agent you run may do without a person approving it.

**An option for computing faculty:** the timeline works as a teaching case on agent safety.

Sources: [AAP via Yahoo News, Nov 10](https://au.news.yahoo.com/ai-safety-watchdog-set-medicare-013534187.html), [The Next Web, Nov 10](https://thenextweb.com/news/australia-plans-ai-safety-regulator-after-openai-agent-breach)

## 5. New top models from Google and Anthropic, at lower prices

{% include story-meta.html for="Entrepreneurs | Businesses" evidence="official" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w46/2026-11-13-fig-4-model-prices" alt="Bars of output prices per million tokens: Claude Fable 5.1 $50, Claude Fable 5.2 $40, Gemini 4 Carbon $10 until Nov 30." caption="Fable's output price fell by a fifth, and Gemini's introductory price doubles after Nov 30." %}

#### What happened

On Nov 10, Google released Gemini 4 Carbon, its new top model, to paying developers through its API and to AI Ultra subscribers. It costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens until Nov 30, then $4 and $20. Tokens are the chunks of text a model reads and writes, and the unit providers bill by. Scientists can apply for six months of free access.

On Nov 9, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50 for Fable 5.1. Anthropic itself suggests starting with its Opus 5.5 model for most tasks. Both companies report benchmark gains they measured themselves.

Lebanon is on [Google's list of countries](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/available-regions.md.txt) for the Gemini API and on [Anthropic's list of supported regions](https://www.anthropic.com/supported-countries).

#### Why it matters

For a small team building on these models, the cost per request fell this week. Google's price doubles after Nov 30, so the sums depend on when you run them.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** the running cost of an AI feature may now be lower than when you priced it.

**Businesses:** build-or-buy comparisons made before this week use old prices.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** price one real workload at Google's rate before and after Nov 30, and at Anthropic's new rate.

**Suggestion for businesses:** compare what a packaged AI tool charges per seat with what the same work would cost per request through a model's API.

Sources: [Google, Nov 10](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude/fable), [MacRumors, Nov 9](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-launches-claude-fable-5-2/)

## 6. When students use AI as a confidant

{% include story-meta.html for="University students | University student-affairs staff" evidence="research (qualitative)" type="New" %}

#### What happened

The Rithm Project interviewed 29 young people about turning to AI for personal support, Inside Higher Ed reported on Nov 10. They were drawn from an earlier survey of about 2,400 people aged 13 to 24, and 19 of them were in college or held a degree.

Nearly all had turned to AI after a specific conflict with another person. AI worked as a "bridge" when they used it to see other points of view or to prepare for a hard conversation. It worked as a "wedge" when they used it only to feel validated.

#### Why it matters

The study looks at personal use of AI, outside coursework, and suggests a plain test: does the use lead back to other people or away from them? Its sample is small and American, and we found no comparable data for Lebanon or the region.

#### How it could affect you

**University students:** the same tool can help you prepare for a hard conversation or stand in for it.

**University student-affairs staff:** the bridge and wedge distinction gives a simple way to talk about AI use with students.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for university students:** if you use AI to think through a conflict, use it to rehearse what you will say, then say it to the person.

**For university student-affairs staff:** the interviews show how students describe this use in their own words.

Source: [Inside Higher Ed, Nov 10](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/health-wellness/2026/11/10/when-ai-becomes-students-confidant)

## For researchers: fake AI papers under real names

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty who publish" evidence="reporting (single source)" type="New" %}

On Nov 9, Times Higher Education reported that three little-known journals had published AI-generated papers credited to professors who did not write them. One, the American Journal of Advanced Multidisciplinary Research and Innovation, ran "dozens" under names including Fei-Fei Li, Christopher Manning, Michael I. Jordan and Stuart Russell. Jure Leskovec of Stanford said he "did not write this paper" and did not authorise the use of his name.

Alan Said of the University of Gothenburg found one under his name through a Google Scholar alert. After he complained it came down, then reappeared under other authors. The three journals' web addresses were all registered in 2026, yet their archives are dated several years back. Fees run from $10 to $200 a paper.

Why it matters: a fake paper under your name can reach citation databases and your public profile. THE gives no total count, and the journals did not reply.

A practice that fits: a Google Scholar alert on your own name shows you new papers credited to you.

Source: [Times Higher Education, Nov 9](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ai-slop-papers-published-under-prominent-academics-names)

### Infra lends HUMAIN up to SAR 2.5 billion

#### What happened

On Nov 8, Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund (Infra) signed two financing agreements with HUMAIN, the national AI company owned by PIF and Aramco. Up to SAR 1.1 billion (about $293 million) is for buying and deploying GPUs, about 25 MW of IT load. Up to SAR 1.4 billion (about $373 million) is for substations, transmission and grid links designed for up to 1 GW of future capacity. They are the first deals under Infra's AI data centre financing programme and use about 56% of a non-binding SAR 4.5 billion framework announced at Davos in January.

#### Why it matters

More than half of the ceiling, SAR 1.4 billion, goes to power and grid links. The amounts are ceilings; the sums drawn and the terms were not disclosed.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** more GPU capacity under a state-backed Saudi provider may change where regional AI products run, and at what price.

**Businesses:** firms that must keep data in Saudi Arabia gain local capacity over time.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** if your product serves Saudi customers, ask providers where its models would run and what that costs.

**Suggestion for businesses:** if you buy AI services in Saudi Arabia, track HUMAIN's capacity next to the Microsoft and AWS cloud regions due by December (watch list).

### Jordan gets $4.2 million for AI in government

#### What happened

Jordan has secured a $4.2 million World Bank grant for AI projects, TechAfrica News reported on Nov 9. It funds AI pilots in government services, opens the Sanad digital identity app to businesses and sets up a National Centre of Excellence in AI. Sources disagree on the signing date (Oct 31 or Nov 4, both just before this window) and on how many people use Sanad.

#### Why it matters

The sum is small and aimed at government use of AI. In purpose it sits close to Lebanon's World Bank digital loan, which still waits in committee (story 1).

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** government AI pilots in Jordan could lead to tenders for local and regional vendors.

**Businesses:** Sanad for businesses could change how firms in Jordan prove identity online.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** if you build for government, follow Jordan's procurement notices for these pilots.

**Suggestion for businesses:** if you operate in Jordan, watch for when Sanad opens to companies.

## Term of the week: risk tier

A risk tier is the band a rule places an AI system in, based on the harm it could do. Higher tiers mean more checks. This week Dubai's financial centre proposed scoring AI that uses personal data on four factors: volume, decision impact, autonomy and sensitivity, each from 1 to 3. A total under 6 is Standard, 6 to 9 is Enhanced, and 10 or more is Critical. A system that makes legally significant decisions about people is Critical whatever it scores.
