Welcome to the first issue of The AI Brief - LB Edition.

## 1. AI found critical PHP flaws, and patching now runs on a shorter clock

{% include story-meta.html for="University staff who run websites | Computing faculty | Entrepreneurs" evidence="official and research" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026-10-29-fig-1-php-timeline" alt="Timeline of the PHP flaws from AI-assisted discovery on Aug 19 to patches on Oct 21, 2026." caption="Attackers were active four days before a patch existed." %}

#### What happened

On Oct 21, the PHP project released fixes for two critical flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. Both let an attacker run code on a web server without logging in. PHP runs about 70% of websites, and Wiz reports that every version released in the past decade has at least one of the bugs.

Wiz and Calif found the flaws with help from AI models. Wiz says it used Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol to find the bugs, build working exploits and check the patches. Details leaked on Oct 20, a day early, and Finland took its public government web services offline as a precaution.

#### Why it matters

The bugs are old. What changed is the speed. Amazon's threat team logged an exploitation attempt on Oct 17, four days before any fix existed. A PHP core developer had spotted the error behind one flaw in a 2024 audit, and it went unfixed because the team couldn't agree on a fix.

#### How it could affect you

**University staff who run websites:** universities across the region run many small sites, such as registration pages, microsites and older WordPress installs. All of them are in scope, including ones set up years ago. Sites on PHP 8.1 or older are the hardest case, because the project no longer ships official fixes for them.

**Computing faculty:** you have a current case with a public timeline, useful for courses on secure software and professional ethics.

**Entrepreneurs:** an online shop or booking site on shared hosting depends on its provider to patch. Attackers could read customer data or take the site over.

#### What you could do

**For university staff who run websites:** the fixed versions are PHP 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 and 8.5.12 or later. CERT-EU advises taking unpatched sites off the public internet until they are fixed.

**An option for computing faculty:** the public timeline works as a case discussion on where each party could have acted differently, including the 2024 decision.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** put the same version question to your host in writing, and update WordPress and its plugins.

Sources: [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/php2shell-php-rce), [CERT-EU](https://cert.europa.eu/publications/security-advisories/2026-018/), [Wikipedia: PHP2Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PHP2Shell)

## 2. Employers say AI makes degrees worth more, if graduates can show judgment

{% include story-meta.html for="University students | University faculty | University advising staff" evidence="research (survey), via reporting" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026-10-29-fig-2-employers-degrees" alt="Bar chart: 48% of US employers say AI makes degrees more important, 26% less important." caption="Nearly twice as many employers expect degrees to matter more as expect them to matter less." %}

#### What happened

Gallup and the Lumina Foundation surveyed 2,500 US employers in August. 48% said AI will make degrees more important over the next five years; 26% said less. 80% prefer a candidate with a degree even when the job doesn't require one. Lumina's Courtney Brown said employers put a premium on judgment, critical thinking, communication and adaptability. Inside Higher Ed reported the results on Oct 20.

#### Why it matters

It is one of the first large employer surveys to push back, with numbers, on the claim that AI makes degrees pointless. It also names what employers say they reward once AI handles routine work. The sample is US only, and stated preferences are not hiring decisions.

#### How it could affect you

**University students:** a finished file says less about you when AI can produce one. Evidence of your reasoning says more.

**University faculty:** assessments that grade only the final output lose some of their signal.

**University career and advising staff:** the figure answers a question parents ask, but quoting it in Lebanon without its US scope would overstate it. For readers at US institutions it applies more directly.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for university students:** keep a short decision note with each major project.

**An option for university faculty:** some use a checkpoint where students explain a choice, such as a two-minute oral defence.

**For university advising staff:** no comparable survey of Lebanese or regional employers has been published, so the US figure needs its scope stated whenever it is quoted.

Source: [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/careers/2026/10/20/employers-say-ai-will-make-college-degrees-more-important)

## 3. Does this AI use strengthen learning, or replace it?

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty | University students" evidence="opinion, grounded in learning research" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026-10-29-fig-3-strengthen-or-replace" alt="Flow chart asking whether an AI use does the thinking for students, leading to redesign or allow." caption="The test turns on one question: would the AI do the thinking the assignment is meant to build?" %}

#### What happened

In an Inside Higher Ed essay on Oct 22, learning researchers Beth M. Schwartz and Christopher Hakala accepted that evidence on AI's learning benefits is thin, and that some studies suggest unguided use can undermine learning. Their answer is to use what the science of learning already shows. One test applies to any AI use: does it strengthen the thinking learning needs (recalling, organizing, explaining, correcting, applying), or do that thinking for the student?

#### Why it matters

Tools change every few weeks; a rule tied to how people learn doesn't. It gives faculty a way to decide now instead of waiting years for studies.

#### How it could affect you

**University faculty:** "AI allowed" and "AI banned" miss the point, because one tool can help in one assignment and hollow out another. This is an argument, not new data, and it hasn't been tested in teaching that moves between Arabic, English and French.

**University students:** AI can make an assignment feel finished while you learn little from it. Exams, interviews and jobs test what stayed with you.

#### What you could do

**An option for university faculty:** the approach the authors describe. Students attempt the work first, then use AI for harder questions or critique, and explain which AI feedback they accepted and why.

**Suggestion for university students:** try the problem yourself before asking AI, then ask it for counterarguments, not answers. Follow each course's AI policy.

Source: [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/opinion/views/2026/10/22/science-learning-should-guide-ai-use-decisions-opinion)

## 4. AI agents that start the work themselves

{% include story-meta.html for="University staff | Entrepreneurs | Businesses" evidence="reporting on company announcements" type="New" %}

#### What happened

On Oct 21, Anthropic opened a US waitlist for Claude Duet, a research preview for paying Pro and Max subscribers. Duet connects to a user's apps, flags what it judges needs attention, keeps working after the user steps away, and asks before acting on the user's behalf. A day later, OpenAI said its models and its Codex coding agent are coming to Google Cloud.

#### Why it matters

A chatbot waits for your question. An agent stays connected to email, calendars and files, and it acts between requests. The question shifts from whether an answer is right to what the agent can touch, and who signs off.

#### How it could affect you

**University staff:** Duet is US only for now, so colleagues based in the US may see it first. Connecting any agent to a work mailbox gives an outside service standing access to what sits there, and no independent security review of Duet has been published.

**Entrepreneurs:** for a team of three, an agent that handles scheduling and follow-ups can free real hours. Regulated customers, such as banks and clinics, will likely ask where their data goes.

**Businesses:** an agent that keeps working between requests puts access and sign-off ahead of answer quality. In banking, health and other regulated sectors, where customer data goes may decide whether an agent can be used at all.

#### What you could do

**For university staff:** an agent with standing access to a work account falls under an employer's rules on outside services, and tasks with no personal data are the lower-risk place to try one.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** write down which actions always need your sign-off (sending, paying, signing) before you switch an agent on.

**Suggestion for businesses:** before any pilot, map which systems an agent could reach and which actions must always wait for a person.

Source: [The Neuron, Oct 22](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-thursday-october-22-2026/)

## 5. Early-stage money is scarcer, and small buyers choose AI on trust

{% include story-meta.html for="Entrepreneurs | Businesses" evidence="research (market data) and opinion" type="Continuing" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026-10-29-fig-4-regional-funding-concentration" alt="Bar showing the UAE and Saudi Arabia took 89% of regional startup funding from January to September 2026." caption="Every other market in the region shared 11% of startup funding." %}

#### What happened

MAGNiTT's data for January to September 2026 shows startups in the Middle East and North Africa raised $2.98 billion, down 7% from a year earlier. Rounds of $100 million or more took $1.5 billion of it, and funding outside those rounds fell 32%. Deal count dropped 33% to 351. The UAE and Saudi Arabia took 89% of the money.

On Oct 19, a Wamda opinion piece by Ahmed Sameh, marketing chief at the software firm Fortis, described how small Gulf businesses adopt AI. In his research, merchants used general tools mostly for marketing copy, and none had tried AI for sales analytics. He cites a Tabby survey finding that 30% of UAE and Saudi shoppers fully trust AI recommendations.

#### Why it matters

Fewer, larger cheques make early rounds harder to raise outside the two biggest markets. And small businesses pick tools that feel safe and simple over tools with long feature lists.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** Lebanese founders raising seed money compete for a narrower pool, much of it regional. Neither source has Lebanon numbers, and the Wamda figures come secondhand from a vendor author.

**Businesses:** in Sameh's research, merchants used general AI tools mostly for marketing copy, and none had tried AI for sales analytics. A Tabby survey he cites found that 30% of UAE and Saudi shoppers fully trust AI recommendations.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** build first features on the customer's own sales data and answer plain questions well (today's sales, best sellers) before offering forecasts. Map regional investors before international ones. None of this guarantees a round; it makes your case easier to check.

**Suggestion for businesses:** start where your own data already sits, such as sales, stock or customer messages, and judge a tool on how well it answers plain questions before paying for forecasts.

Sources: [Arab News on MAGNiTT data](https://www.arabnews.com/startups/startup-wrap-mega-rounds-sustain-mena-funding-as-saudi-arabia-powers-quarterly-rebound-3006576), [MAGNiTT](https://www.magnitt.com/research/Q3-2026-MENA-VC-Update-51060), [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/10/trust-ai-adoption-really-looks-like-gulf-smes)

## For researchers: what journal AI rules now say

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty who publish or review" evidence="reporting (a third-party policy tracker)" type="Background" %}

Manusights, a site that tracks journal AI policies, has compared 49 publisher and society policies. 45 rule out listing AI as an author, and none allows it. 44 require authors to disclose AI use, most often in the Methods section (31 policies), then Acknowledgments (20). 29 bar reviewers from uploading manuscripts to AI tools, and 7 allow it only with approved or secure tools. Its mid-October change log notes that Cambridge's reviewer rule treats sharing manuscript content with any AI tool, without the journal's agreement, as peer review misconduct.

Why it matters: the rules are converging but still differ by journal, and a disclosure written for one journal may not fit the next.

A practice that fits most of these policies: keep a short record for each paper of which tool you used, what it did and how you checked it. When you move a paper to another journal, rewrite the disclosure for that journal. As a reviewer, keep manuscripts out of AI tools unless the journal says otherwise. Check the journal's own instructions, since this is a secondary tracker.

Source: [Manusights tracker](https://manusights.com/blog/journal-ai-policies-2026)

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs and researchers:** track when capacity comes online, not when it is announced, using the table above.

## Term of the week: coordinated disclosure

Coordinated disclosure means reporting a security flaw privately to the people who maintain the software, so they can build a fix before details go public. Researchers and maintainers agree on a release date, and patches ship with the announcement. This week's PHP flaws show the system under strain. AI helped find the bugs, details leaked a day early, and attackers were probing before patches shipped. The time between disclosure and attack keeps shrinking.
