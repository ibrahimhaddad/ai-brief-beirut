## Editor's note

This issue covers eleven days, Oct 26 to Nov 5, as the newsletter moves to Fridays. From next week, each issue covers Friday to Thursday.

Lebanon leads. The bill creating a Ministry of Technology and Artificial Intelligence cleared a parliamentary subcommittee on Oct 27. It is the first step; a plenary vote comes next. The minister says himself that the digital economy depends on two other laws, on personal data and cybersecurity, and both are still drafts.

Most of the other stories are about control: who sets the terms once AI is inside an institution. Cornell and Princeton published the terms of their campus licences, and Abu Dhabi signed AI deals that keep the processing in the country.

Each story says who it is for and what evidence sits behind it. If you only have two minutes, pick your page above.

Ibrahim Haddad

## 1. Lebanon's AI ministry bill clears committee, while data and cyber laws wait

{% include story-meta.html for="Businesses | Entrepreneurs | Law and computing faculty" evidence="reporting on official statements" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w45/2026-11-06-fig-1-ministry-bill-timeline" alt="Timeline of Lebanon's AI ministry bill from Decree 1286 in Sep 2025 to a plenary vote with no date yet." caption="The subcommittee step is done; the joint committees meet on Nov 9 and the plenary vote has no date." %}

#### What happened

On Oct 27, a subcommittee of Parliament's joint committees approved the draft law creating a Ministry of Technology and Artificial Intelligence. MP Tony Frangieh, who chaired it, said the work took three sessions and called it "the completion of the first phase." The bill goes next to a plenary vote. No date has been set.

The government sent the bill to Parliament by Decree 1286 of Sep 19, 2025, and the plenary referred it to the joint committees on Jul 15, 2026. A notice dated Nov 6 calls the joint committees to continue studying it on Nov 9.

In a radio interview reported on Oct 30, Kamal Shehadi, Minister of State for Technology and AI, called the plenary vote the real milestone. He said three concerns came up in the debate: overlap with other ministries, whether the new ministry would have enough authority, and privacy, data protection and cybersecurity. A digital economy, he said, depends on passing a personal data protection law and a cybersecurity law.

He made a related point on Oct 27 at Beirut's Sixth Regional Conference on Cybersecurity in the Age of AI. A draft cybersecurity law that would create a national authority is being finalised, he said.

A day later, Fadi Makki, Minister of State for Administrative Reform, called for coordination between ministries to avoid "overlapping AI initiatives." His office, OMSAR, runs the World Bank's Lebanon Digital Acceleration Project: $150 million from the World Bank's lending arm (IBRD) plus $35 million in commercial financing.

#### Why it matters

As Shehadi describes it, the ministry would cover digital infrastructure, digital transformation and skills, and carry on his office's work on legislation and coordination with other ministries. The bill's text is not public, so its powers and budget are not known yet.

The rules companies would actually work under sit in the two other laws he named. Until they pass, a new ministry could set direction, but the data and security rules that AI projects handling personal data need would still be missing.

#### How it could affect you

**Businesses:** banks, insurers, retailers and others that hold customer data face two laws still in draft. What they will say about AI, and about data sent abroad, is not public.

**Entrepreneurs:** the World Bank project has a component for digitising high-impact public services, a likely source of government tenders. Makki said the Dawlati portal will help decide which services go first.

**Law and computing faculty:** the debate is a live case of a state deciding where AI policy should sit, and with what powers.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for businesses:** keep a one-page note of where customer data is stored, who can reach it, and which services send it abroad. Both draft laws turn on those questions.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** if you build for government, follow the procurement notices for World Bank project P506791.

**An option for law and computing faculty:** the three concerns raised in the debate work as a seminar prompt, set against how other countries have placed AI policy.

Sources: [Daily Beirut, Oct 27](https://dailybeirut.com/en/lebanon-news/parliamentary-committee-approves-bill-establish-ministry-technology-artificial-i/), [MTV, Oct 30](https://www.mtv.com.lb/news/1753409), [Intelligent CIO, Oct 28](https://www.intelligentcio.com/me/2026/10/28/lebanon-advances-us150-million-government-digital-transformation-and-ai-strategy/)

## 2. An AI review beat human reviewers in a Harvard test, as Springer Nature builds one

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty who publish or review" evidence="research (preprint, not peer reviewed) and official" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w45/2026-11-06-fig-2-ai-review-flow" alt="Flow: paper, journal AI review, author response, editor decision, with human review only when needed." caption="In this proposal, editors call in a human reviewer only when they judge it necessary." %}

#### What happened

Gary King and Steven Worthington of Harvard's Institute for Quantitative Social Science compared 134 human reviews of 124 papers, written for the institute's paid pre-submission service, with reviews written by Anthropic's Claude Opus. Three former journal editors ranked 10 random pairs and preferred the AI review 87% of the time. With OpenAI's GPT-5.5 as the judge, the AI review won every pair. It scored higher on all seven criteria.

On Oct 26, Springer Nature and Google DeepMind said they will co-design a review assistant built on Gemini. It flags passages that may need closer scrutiny and runs only on Springer Nature's private platform. A prototype is in testing, with no release date.

#### Why it matters

Most journal rules still treat AI in peer review as something to keep out. King and Worthington propose that some journals stop routine human review. Editors would weigh the paper, the journal's AI review and the author's response, and commission a human review only when needed. They warn this could push science toward conventional ideas and give AI companies more say over what counts as good work.

The study is not peer reviewed, and 10 pairs is a small sample. Still, the same week a large publisher began building AI into its own process.

#### How it could affect you

**University faculty who publish or review:** reviews of your papers may soon be drafted or screened in part by AI that the journal controls. That differs from a reviewer pasting your manuscript into a public chatbot, which many journals still bar.

#### What you could do

**An option for university faculty who publish or review:** the study's seven criteria (sources, weaknesses, methods, theory and ideas, framing, communication, figures and tables) make a checklist for your next review.

Sources: [Inside Higher Ed, Nov 5](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty/books-publishing/2026/11/05/ai-article-review-better-humans-their-best), [Times Higher Education, Oct 26](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/google-deepmind-and-springer-nature-create-ai-peer-review-tool), [Springer Nature release (Japanese)](https://www.natureasia.com/ja-jp/info/press-releases/detail/9469)

## 3. Universities start buying AI for everyone on campus

{% include story-meta.html for="University staff | University faculty | University students" evidence="official and reporting" type="New" %}

#### What happened

On Oct 26, Cornell announced a two-year pilot that gives its community several AI tools: ChatGPT and Codex, Claude Cowork and Claude Code, Microsoft Copilot Chat and, tentatively, Gemini. Faculty, staff and PhD students get access in November, master's students and undergraduates in early 2027. Base access is free and data cannot be used to train models. Faculty and staff take a responsible-use orientation, and a further primer before using agentic tools (AI that carries out tasks on its own).

Princeton announced a one-year ChatGPT pilot on Oct 28. Students can request accounts from January. ChatGPT Plus, normally $20 a month, is free for students. For Pro, normally $100, departments pay $50. Neither university disclosed what it pays.

#### Why it matters

A campus-wide licence removes the gap between students who pay for strong tools and those who don't. Cornell chose several vendors because, in its words, "no single AI model is best for every discipline or task." Both are US cases, with no reported equivalent in Lebanon or the Gulf this week.

#### How it could affect you

**University staff:** these terms become a reference point for AI licensing. They apply first to readers at US institutions.

**University faculty:** when every student has the same paid tool, assumptions about who has access to what change.

**University students:** a university-licensed account and a free personal one come with different data terms.

#### What you could do

**For university staff:** the two sets of terms (no training on data, required training, discounted extra capacity) are a ready comparison for any AI contract.

**An option for university faculty:** Cornell's new research integrity policy, which covers AI use in research, is a public example of how a university writes AI rules for research.

**Suggestion for university students:** before uploading coursework or research data, check whether the tool you are using is a personal account or a licensed one.

Sources: [Cornell Chronicle, Oct 26](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/10/seat-table-cornell-expands-access-multiple-ai-tools-faculty-staff-students), [Cornell Daily Sun, Oct 28](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/10/cornell-to-offer-free-chatgpt-claude-and-gemini-access-for-students-staff), [Daily Princetonian, Oct 28](https://www.dailyprincetonian.com/article/2026/10/princeton-news-breaking-adpol-princeton-to-provide-chatgpt-accounts-all-students-faculty-staff)

## 4. Abu Dhabi and Dubai hand government work to AI agents, with data kept in the UAE

{% include story-meta.html for="Businesses | Entrepreneurs" evidence="reporting on official announcements" type="New" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w45/2026-11-06-fig-3-rta-targets" alt="Bars of Dubai RTA targets: complaints 20 to 30% faster, delivery 15 to 20% and procurement 10 to 15% shorter." caption="These are targets over 2.5 years; the one reported result so far is Copilot use." %}

#### What happened

On Nov 2, Abu Dhabi signed four agreements with OpenAI. ADNOC, the state energy company, will use OpenAI models through Inception42, a G42 company, with inference (the step where a model answers a request) running on servers inside the UAE. A government programme will turn selected regulations into machine-readable form for routine checks such as permits. Two more cover Mubadala's investment analysis and a five-year Inception42 deal across government, enterprise and education. No financial terms were disclosed.

A day earlier, Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) updated its AI strategy. It aims to move 50% of its operations and services to agentic AI and to add more than AED 300 million a year in value, with 40 AI products planned and 11 done.

#### Why it matters

In-country inference answers the first question regulated buyers ask about frontier models: where the data goes. The Abu Dhabi programme also sets a governance pattern: each AI assessment traces back to the regulation it applies, and an official makes the final decision. A pilot on food imports is running.

The RTA figures are targets. The one reported result: more than 90% of staff with Copilot use it, saving about 10% of their working time.

#### How it could affect you

**Businesses:** UAE banks, energy firms and government suppliers can now buy frontier AI processed inside the country. In Lebanon, the data protection and cybersecurity laws that would set comparable rules are still drafts (story 1).

**Entrepreneurs:** UAE buyers now have an in-country option to measure other vendors against.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for businesses:** for an internal AI pilot, tie each AI output to the rule or record it relies on, and name who signs off.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** if you sell to UAE government or regulated firms, be ready to say where your AI processing runs.

Sources: [Khaleej Times, Nov 2](https://khaleejtimes.com/uae/abu-dhabi-crown-prince-openai-altman-4-deals-key-sectors), [Khaleej Times, Nov 1](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-rta-targets-traffic-congestion-cut-ai-strategy), [Middle East AI News, Nov 2](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/abu-dhabi-signs-deals-with-openai)

## 5. Data rules tighten in Egypt and Saudi Arabia

{% include story-meta.html for="Businesses | Entrepreneurs" evidence="reporting (law-firm analysis of official texts)" type="New" %}

#### What happened

Egypt's one-year grace period under its personal data protection law, Law No. 151 of 2020, ended around Nov 1, a year after its executive regulations were issued. No extension was announced, according to the law firm Belex. The law covers foreign entities that handle Egyptians' data. Obligations include licences, a registered data protection officer, processing records and a separate licence or permit for each cross-border transfer. Breaches go to the regulator within 72 hours.

In Saudi Arabia, the consultation on draft amendments to the data protection rules from SDAIA, the data authority, closed on Nov 5. One draft article would require controllers to store personal data inside the Kingdom, while still allowing transfers abroad. Others would require impact assessments for emerging technologies and for automated decisions that affect people's rights, and privacy notices in the language normally used with customers.

#### Why it matters

Two of the region's largest markets now ask where data sits and who approves its transfer. When a company's AI tools send customer data to models hosted abroad, that becomes a design question as much as a legal one. The Saudi rules are drafts. Once published in the official gazette, they take effect after 60 days.

#### How it could affect you

**Businesses:** a Lebanese firm serving Egyptian customers can fall under Egypt's law without an office there.

**Entrepreneurs:** a product that sends Saudi customers' personal data to an AI model abroad may need a legal basis for the transfer, and an Arabic privacy notice if Arabic is the language you use with those customers.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for businesses:** list the cloud regions and AI services that hold your Egyptian and Saudi customers' data.

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** build Arabic privacy notices and an impact assessment for automated decisions into the product while it is small. General information only; a lawyer can confirm how the rules apply to you.

Sources: [Belex, Oct 26](https://www.belex.com/en/case_study/egypts-personal-data-protection-law-the-grace-period-ends-on-31-october-2026-is-your-business-ready/), [Clyde & Co, Oct 8](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/sdaia-publishes-draft-amendments-to-the-pdpl), [Gowling WLG, Oct 16](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/saudi-arabias-proposed-pdpl-amendments)

## 6. PHP2Shell moves from warning to confirmed attacks

{% include story-meta.html for="University staff who run websites | Businesses that run their own websites | Computing faculty" evidence="official and research" type="Continuing" %}

{% include figure.html file="2026w45/2026-11-06-fig-4-php2shell-funnel" alt="Bars narrowing from about 19,000 firewalls found to 240 targeted and 25 taken over in a PHP2Shell campaign." caption="Of 240 targeted firewalls, 25 were taken over with full control." %}

#### What happened

On Oct 26, the US cybersecurity agency CISA added both PHP2Shell flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, to its catalog of vulnerabilities known to be exploited. Issue 0 covered their discovery. Dream Research then documented a campaign that combined PHP2Shell with a Linux kernel flaw to take full control of 25 Palo Alto Networks firewall and management servers, three of them in a US government-affiliated network. The attacker found about 19,000 internet-facing firewalls and targeted 240. Palo Alto's Unit 42 team says it verified the findings.

The fixed PHP versions are unchanged: 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 and 8.5.12. The Dutch national cyber security centre, NCSC-NL, tracks 757 products. On Oct 28, 218 of them were vulnerable and 144 had fixes.

#### Why it matters

Two weeks ago the risk was a warning. Now exploitation is confirmed, and it reaches past websites, because security appliances can carry vulnerable PHP too. A third, related flaw disclosed on Oct 23 had no fix by the end of this window.

#### How it could affect you

**University staff who run websites:** the exposure is sector-wide and includes appliances as well as old sites.

**Businesses that run their own websites:** a firewall or other appliance on your network can carry vulnerable PHP even when your website does not.

**Computing faculty:** the campaign is a documented chain of two flaws, with public numbers at each step.

#### What you could do

**For university staff who run websites:** CISA's catalog and the NCSC-NL tracker are the public references on what is affected and what has been fixed.

**Suggestion for businesses:** check the NCSC-NL tracker for the vendor products you run, firewalls included.

**An option for computing faculty:** the narrowing from 19,000 devices found to 25 taken over makes a case study in how attackers choose targets.

Sources: [CISA, Oct 26](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCISA/bulletins/4309083), [Dream Research](https://dreamgroup.com/blog/chained-php2shell-exploit-roots-25-servers), [NCSC-NL tracker](https://github.com/NCSC-NL/php2shell)

## For researchers: a delisted journal's retractions pass 250

{% include story-meta.html for="University faculty who publish" evidence="reporting" type="New" %}

Retraction Watch reported on Nov 3 that the Taylor & Francis journal Bioengineered has retracted 119 more papers, bringing its total to 255 since an investigation began in 2021. More than 1,000 of its articles received "under investigation" notices in July 2025. Clarivate put the journal on hold in 2024 and removed it from Web of Science in May 2025. Most notices cite "significant concerns" about data integrity. The main suspicion is paper mills, businesses that sell fabricated or manipulated papers.

Why it matters: the notices do not mention AI. The case matters for anyone choosing a journal or reading a publication list. A paper in a journal that is later delisted can lose standing years after it appeared.

A practice that fits: before submitting, check that the journal is still indexed in Web of Science or Scopus, and look at its recent retraction notices.

Source: [Retraction Watch, Nov 3](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/11/03/bioengineered-retracts-119-more-papers-as-investigation-continues)

### Aramco takes 49% of Humain

#### What happened

Aramco closed a deal for 49% of Humain, Saudi Arabia's national AI company, Wamda reported on Nov 2. Aramco contributes about $2.6 billion (SAR 9.9 billion) in cash during 2026, plus its own AI, compute and digital assets. PIF keeps 51%, and no valuation was disclosed. Humain targets 3 GW of AI data centre capacity by 2030. In the same week it took control of CNTXT, Google Cloud's reseller in Saudi Arabia, which serves more than 400 corporate and government customers.

#### Why it matters

Humain now has two state owners, and one of them is also a customer: Aramco plans to run AI across its operations on Humain's infrastructure. With CNTXT, Humain adds cloud resale and enterprise services to its compute.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** more Saudi enterprise AI buying may now run through one national provider that has compute, cloud resale and an anchor customer under one roof.

**Businesses:** firms in Saudi Arabia get a local provider that can sell compute and Google Cloud services together.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** if you sell AI services in Saudi Arabia, work out where your product sits against Humain's offer: as a partner, a supplier or a competitor.

**Suggestion for businesses:** if you buy cloud or AI services in Saudi Arabia, add a provider that bundles local compute with Google Cloud resale to the options you price.

### BY Venture Partners closes a $60 million AI fund

#### What happened

BY Venture Partners, a Dubai-based firm with Lebanese roots, closed its third fund at $60 million on Oct 29, Wamda reported. Dubai Future District Fund anchored it, joined by MultiLane, Transmed Capital and the Saudi Idriss Family Office. The firm writes cheques of up to $1 million into AI-native startups from pre-seed to Series A, with a focus on enterprise workflow tools. It has made 15 of a planned 40 investments from the fund and manages more than $200 million.

#### Why it matters

Early-stage money has been scarce in the region. In MAGNiTT data for January to September 2026, funding outside rounds of $100 million or more fell 32%. A fund writing cheques of up to $1 million into AI startups adds to that pool. About two-thirds of its capital goes outside the region, mainly to North America and Europe.

#### How it could affect you

**Entrepreneurs:** Lebanese founders building enterprise workflow tools fit the fund's stated focus, though most of its money is set to go to companies outside the region.

#### What you could do

**Suggestion for entrepreneurs:** set the fund's stated focus (AI-native, enterprise workflow, pre-seed to Series A) against your own stage before you pitch. A fit with the thesis can open a conversation; the cheque depends on the fund's own review.

### The wider picture

## Term of the week: in-country inference

Inference is the moment an AI model answers a request: your text goes in, the model runs on a server, and an answer comes back. In-country inference means that step happens on servers inside a given country, so the data in the request stays there. This week Abu Dhabi signed OpenAI deals that run on GPUs in UAE data centres, and Saudi draft rules would require personal data to be stored in the Kingdom. For banks and governments, where processing happens can decide whether a tool is allowed at all.
